## The regulatory driver — why now (from the pitch deck)

As of **March 2026**, Banco Central's **Resolução CMN nº 5.267/2025**
requires rural-credit compliance to be proven by remote sensing.
Outsourcing the check does not outsource the liability — quoting the
regulation directly (BCB, MCR 2-9):

> *"A responsabilidade legal e regulatória pela conformidade continua
> sendo indelegavelmente dela."*
> ("Legal and regulatory responsibility for compliance remains
> non-delegable.")

This is the single most important market-timing fact in this data room:
it converts "nice to have geospatial data" into "a bank now has a
standing legal obligation it cannot contract away, and needs a vendor
who can prove compliance in an auditable, signed format." That's exactly
what the Passaporte de Crédito Rural's MCR 2-9 dossier (ICP-Brasil
signed) is built to be — see `09-product/PRODUCT_OVERVIEW.md`.

## Supporting bottom-up sizing (IBGE / Plano Safra, sourced independently)

Kept as corroborating detail — a second, independently-sourced angle
that arrives in the same order of magnitude as the deck's own figures,
which is a useful thing to be able to say in the room.

- IBGE's PAM 2025 (all 70 tracked crops): **101.3 million hectares**
  planted nationally.
  [IBGE](https://ftp.ibge.gov.br/Producao_Agricola/Levantamento_Sistematico_da_Producao_Agricola_%5Bmensal%5D/Fasciculo_Indicadores_IBGE/2025/estProdAgri_202508.pdf)
- IBGE's 2026 estimate for cereals/legumes/oilseeds: **83.1 million
  hectares**, up 1.8% y/y.
  [Jornal de Brasília](https://jornaldebrasilia.com.br/noticias/economia/safra-2026-alcancara-353-milhoes-de-t-2-maior-que-a-de-2025-afirma-ibge/)
- Rural credit this plugs into — **Plano Safra 2025/26: R$ 516.2
  billion** available; **Plano Safra 2026/27: R$ 525.1 billion**.
  [Grupo Amanhã](https://amanha.com.br/categoria/brasil/plano-safra-2025-2026-disponibilizara-r-516-2-bilhoes) ·
  [O Especialista / Safra](https://oespecialista.safra.com.br/plano-safra-2026-2027-credito-rural-525-bilhoes/)
- At 101.3M ha × R$4/ha (US$0.80), a full-market subscription TAM would
  be **≈ R$405M/year (≈US$81M/year)** — in the same order of magnitude
  as the deck's US$880M figure once one-off services, MRV, and
  enterprise revenue are layered on top of the pure subscription base.

## The R$800bn fraud-exposure figure (problem-sizing, not market-sizing)

Separately, the deck's problem slide states **R$ 800 billion (≈ US$ 157
billion) in active Brazilian rural credit is exposed to four fraud
vectors** the current paper-based process can't catch: falsified land
area, grain diverted before loan maturity, judicial-recovery used as a
collection shield, and deforested land laundered through a "clean" CAR.
This is a different number from the TAM above — it's the size of the
*risk* the product mitigates, not the size of the *addressable revenue*.
Keep these two numbers distinct when presenting; conflating them
overstates the market opportunity.
