### 2. Capacity Reality Check

**The honest math.** ARPAC's ~70,000 ha/year across 8 bases means a pilot is already stationed near the client when the weather window opens. Our model requires dispatching a partner pilot per mission: travel days, hotel, SARPAS filing, then flight. On response time for ad-hoc missions, they win structurally today.

**The real constraint is not pilot supply.** The report is clear (Section 11): partner pilots are abundant — thousands of new DJI operators need demand. The binding constraints, in order:

1. **QA throughput and standardization** — a third-party pilot we've never flown with is a rework risk. My rework KPI is ≤3% (OKR KR3.4 tightens it to ≤2%); uncontrolled third-party capture is how we blow it.
2. **The 48h delivery SLA** — the clock includes raw-data transfer from a pilot in the field (rural connectivity), outsourced processing (R$ 1.90/ha/product), and my QA checklist. Each handoff is a failure point we don't fully control.
3. **SARPAS authorizations** — every mission needs DECEA clearance under the pilot's registration; a disorganized partner delays the mission before the drone leaves the case.
4. **Weather windows** — same for everyone (winds <8 m/s, no rain), but ARPAC's stationed bases can re-attempt next morning; our pilot may have a 400 km deadhead. Weather amplifies every other constraint for us.

**Where asset-light can match density:** *scheduled, clustered, recurring* work — the Safra/Profissional 3-flights-per-season programs. Planned campaigns let me book pilots weeks ahead, cluster neighboring farms, and pre-file SARPAS. Where we cannot match: urgent single-farm flights on 72h notice. Sales must sell programs, not emergencies.

### 3. What the Asset-Light Model Needs to Work

- **Partner-pilot pipeline (recruit → certify → activate):** documented onboarding gate — valid ANAC IN 86/2021 registration, SISANT drone registry, SARPAS proficiency, insurance, one paid test flight QA'd against our checklist before any client mission. Target: a certified bench of 5-8 pilots covering the GO/SP corridor. The DJI operator wave supplies candidates; certification is our job.
- **Standardized flight + QA SOPs:** one flight-parameter spec (overlap, GSD, altitude, sensor config), one data-handoff protocol, one acceptance checklist. No delivery leaves without passing it. This feeds KR5.1 (100% critical SOPs documented — currently 0%).
- **48h SLA re-engineering:** the SLA must start at *validated data receipt*, with an internal pilot-upload deadline of ≤12h post-flight written into the partner contract, with payment tied to it. Otherwise I cannot sign KR3.2 (100% ≤48h).
- **Logistics amortization by clustering:** R$ 4.00/ha logistics drops toward R$ 3.00 at ≥5,000 ha clusters (Unit Economics §1). Operations needs visibility into the 93-prospect GO/SP list to route pilots through neighboring fornecedores in one trip — this is a scheduling discipline, and it's also margin lever #3.

### 4. Service-Level Competition

**Where we can beat them:** delivery product quality (5 products per flight, ROI-framed reports — ARPAC's analytics layer is thin per the report); price flexibility with zero fleet capex; carbon/audit-grade documentation nobody in the matrix offers; elastic peak capacity — I can add pilots for harvest season without buying aircraft.

**Where we cannot, and I won't claim we can:** response time on unplanned missions in their 8-base footprint; continuous-presence photogrammetry at a usina; spraying frequency as a relationship wedge. Sales kill-sheets must not promise "faster than ARPAC."

### 5. Risks I'm Flagging

1. **Safety dilution:** third-party pilots operating under our brand with zero incidents required (KR3.3). One uncertified shortcut ends the KPI and possibly the company.
2. **Compliance exposure:** a partner flying without SARPAS authorization or lapsed ANAC registration creates liability we cannot transfer contractually in reputation terms.
3. **Quality variance under scaling:** rework >3% at partner scale destroys the 48h SLA and the R$ 80 Pacote margin simultaneously.
4. **Single-point QA bottleneck:** all QA currently runs through me/Chief Pilot; at 15 new contracts (KR2.1) it saturates.
5. **Data custody:** raw data on partner pilots' devices — our season-over-season moat requires contractual data ownership and immediate transfer.

### 2. Margin & Pricing Risk Assessment

**(a) DaaS spraying — the band is invalid; here is the arithmetic.** Formula: net margin = (0.85·P − C_pilot)/P, so a 25% floor requires **C_pilot ≤ 0.60·P** (walk-away 15% requires C_pilot ≤ 0.70·P). Against the report's row-crop market (soy/corn R$20–80, realistically R$20–50):

| Service price P | Max pilot+logistics cost for 25% margin | For 15% walk-away |
|---|---|---|
| R$35/ha | R$21.00 | R$24.50 |
| R$50/ha | R$30.00 | R$35.00 |
| R$60/ha | R$36.00 | R$42.00 |

So intermediation only clears the healthy floor if we contract spraying pilots at **≤ R$21–36/ha all-in** across the report's suggested soy band (R$35–60). Our UNIT_ECONOMICS pilot costs (R$11.50 + R$4.00 logistics = R$15.50/ha) are *mapping* costs — unvalidated for spraying, where the pilot IS the service. The report itself flags "pilot cost ≤ R$25–35/ha — validate before selling." Until we have three signed partner-pilot rate cards, DaaS revenue is R$0 in my base case.

**(b) Mapping commoditization (R$40–60/ha above 100 ha).** Stress test at standard COGS R$17.40 (1 flight/1 product): Essencial R$35 → (29.75 − 17.40)/35 = **35.3% margin** — safe, and actually *below* the published market, so no defensive cut is warranted. The 25% floor sits at P ≥ 17.40/0.60 = **R$29.00**; our approved R$30 volume floor yields 27.0% — it holds, with only R$1/ha of buffer at standard costs. At locked volume costs (COGS R$14.90) the floor price is R$24.83 and R$30 yields 35.3%. This is exactly the **Bruno Luiz lesson**: R$25/ha at degraded costs was break-even after tax; the R$30 floor is non-negotiable, and it must always ship with the volume-cost precondition (pilot R$10 + logistics R$3, ≥10k ha). Below 100 ha we are underpricing: market carries R$60–90/ha — I support the small-area tier.

**(c) Recurring NDVI exposure.** The R$2.5–15k/month line is the satellite-killable product. KR2.5 targets 3 annual monitoring contracts; at that band, those represent **R$7.5k–45k of MRR — i.e., 9% to 56% of our R$80k KR1.1 target**, midpoint ~R$26k (~33%). One-third of the MRR objective resting on a product with a free substitute is unacceptable concentration risk. Mitigation is the report's own: bundle satellite between drone flights and sell verification, not pixels.
